1 / 5

Hundreds of experts, one model.

A mixture-of-experts model is a crowd of small specialists. Each arc is one device’s share of them, sized by the memory its owner lends.

2 / 5

The core stays on the server.

Attention, the router, the shared experts and the conversation’s memory live where memory is widest. At every layer the router picks 8 of 256 experts for the token.

3 / 5

Only the chosen experts wake.

A few kilobytes of hidden state go to the devices that hold them and come back. The most-called experts stay on the server: in S0’s traces the hottest quarter took two thirds of the calls.

4 / 5

Every device joins the pool.

Old phones, laptops, desktops, GPU boxes and servers join one pool. Each takes the part its memory, chip and link can carry; none is left out.

5 / 5

Measured, not promised.

Every speed Omnarc shows comes from a run on real devices. In S0 one expert step cost 0.9 ms inside one machine, 28 ms to a phone over Wi-Fi and about 500 ms across the internet.

Memory each owner lends, and its bandwidth.

A token calls k of E experts at each of L layers. The core keeps the hottest share x of them, which in S0’s routing traces answered a share h(x) of the calls. A layer waits on the network unless all its k experts are on the core, as in a share s(x) of the steps:

W = L · ( 1 − s(x) ),   tnet = W · tstep

h and s come from GLM-4.7 Flash (4 experts a token) and Granite 3B (8 a token); tstep from the three S0 links.

03The math

      
Three formulas behind the pool.

      
Each one decides a design choice in Omnarc. Move the sliders and watch the trade-off.

    

    

      

        

          
Speculative decoding

          
A small draft model guesses k tokens; the full model checks them in one pass. With acceptance rate α, tokens gained per pass:

          
E[n] = 1 − αk+11 − α,   speed-up = E[n]1 + k·c

          
        

        

          
          
          

        

      

      

        

          
Replicas and uptime

          
Each of G expert groups lives on r devices that are each online with probability p. The pool answers when every group has a live copy:

          
Ppool = ( 1 − (1 − p)r )G

          
        

        

          
          
          

        

      

      

        

          
Why only mixture-of-experts

          
Generation reads every active weight once per token, so speed is capped by memory bandwidth β over the bytes read. A dense model reads all S of them; a mixture-of-experts model reads only its active share a:

          
tok/s ≤ βa · S

          
        

        

          
          
          

        

      

    

  







  

    

      
04who is it for

      
One mind for what you do.

      
Goals of the plan — stages S2, S3 and S4.

    

    

      
S3
Agents on your own model
OpenCode, OpenClaw and other agents run on a large open mixture-of-experts model spread across your own computers — no per-token bill, no rate limit. Stage S3 passes when exactly that works on a fixed task set.

      
S2
The family pool
A family or a team pools every device it has, old phones included, and one assistant answers everyone at once, in their own language.

      
S4
The night office
Accounts, quotas and schedules: the office computers serve the team by day and run long jobs at night, with an audit trail.

08questions

      
Questions

    

      
Is Omnarc ready to use?
Not yet; it is pre-alpha. Stage S0 is under way: we measure what already exists — plain llama.cpp and Mesh LLM — on real devices before building on it. The golden path, with one installer and a QR invite, is stage S1.

      
Are the numbers on this page real?
The numbers credited to S0 were measured on our own laptop, phone and server; bench/s0 lists the hardware, versions and commands. The pool lab is an estimate built from those measurements, and the hero and the rack are animations.

      
Does my data leave my devices?
Your text reaches only your pool’s server, a machine you own or run. Expert devices see hidden states for the layers they hold, never your words. Links run inside an encrypted tunnel, and there is no telemetry.

      
Why only mixture-of-experts models?
Generation reads every active weight once per token. A dense model reads all of them, so splitting it across devices only adds network waits: our own split of a dense 14B model gave 0.37 tokens/s. A mixture-of-experts model reads a small part per token — DeepSeek V3 calls 8 of its 256 experts — so its experts can live on many ordinary devices.

      
Can my old phone really help?
Yes. No device is left out: each takes the part its memory, chip and link can carry — a few experts, a cache, a copy. In S0 a 32-bit phone held the experts of eight layers over Wi-Fi, and the answer stayed the same, byte for byte.

      
Is it free?
Yes. Apache 2.0, no account, no telemetry. The compute is yours and is never sold; what may be sold later is what surrounds it for organizations — accounts, quotas, audit — and only after stage S4.
